      Site plans

      Brand and interiors

      Best for
      Designers and specifiers, upmarket homes, and brands that sell on the look and feel of their materials
      Build effort
      Low to medium

      Visitors go from a finished-room photo into the collection, then to close-ups of the tile surface, the trims and the samples. For interior photography see Mutina, Karimoku Case and Henge; for the catalog, Cosentino; for how components fit together, Vitsœ; for booking an appointment, Reform.

      Page modules
      1. Room photo in the heroA real installed photo where the joints, light and surface are clear, with “View the collection” and “Book a sample appointment” next to it.
      2. Collection entry pointsGroup collections by material (warm stone, mineral rock, handmade glaze…), show how they differ, then lead to the products.
      3. Visual material catalogLarge texture swatches with a few key filters: color family, finish, size, where it can be used. Active filters stay visible.
      4. Room and detail side by sideEach collection has a full room, a close-up of the surface and an edge detail, so visitors can compare the effect in a room with the texture of the tile.
      5. Specs and matching piecesField tiles, decor tiles, baseboards and trims are explained within the same collection, with links to specs, care and samples.
      6. Sample appointmentsOnline advice or a showroom visit, prefilled with the collections the visitor browsed; area and dates are asked only when needed.

      What it takesMost of the work is art direction, interior photography, material scans, catalog content and consistent layout. Start with images, short videos and light interaction; a template cannot make up for weak photos.

      Selection and quotes Do this first

      Best for
      The main tile website, for homeowners and trade clients alike
      Build effort
      Medium to high

      The customer picks a floor tile, tries it on a photo of their room, saves the result and sends it all to sales. For the catalog and project list see Cosentino and Halcyon; for the photo try-on, Roomvo and Tilesview. For the shopping assistant, look at Mylow, Magic Apron and Zalando.

      Page modules
      1. Start from the projectBathroom, kitchen, living room or outdoor, each opening the catalog that fits; or search a model number directly.
      2. Clear product cards and comparisonShow size, finish and the performance data that matters. Compare 2–3 products field by field, with traceable units and standards.
      3. Upload a photo, see real productsDetect walls and floors and let the user correct them; switch between real SKUs; keep the before/after view, the laying pattern and the grout color.
      4. An AI assistant with contextIt knows the current room, the shortlisted products and the confirmed requirements. Its suggestions become editable filters, and each reason points to the official product data.
      5. Saved projectPhotos, SKUs, laying pattern, open questions and options A/B share one project record.
      6. Quantities you can check, then the quoteConfirmed area, area per box, waste allowance and accessories are calculated separately; at submission the customer only adds contact and delivery details.

      What it takesBuild structured SKUs, filters, comparison and the project list first; add the photo preview and AI search once they have been tested. The work is in the assets, the usage rules, stock and prices, and a way for staff to correct things by hand.

      Flagship exhibition

      Best for
      Launches, design weeks and flagship collections with a strong story
      Build effort
      High

      One collection tells the story from raw material to firing to finished tile. See how IST shows the production steps, how Cartier moves between exhibition rooms, and how Moooi and Igloo build their scenes. Oryzo’s macro shots work as a storyboard reference. Every piece on display links to its specs and to booking.

      Page modules
      1. Opening shotOne key visual move: the grain of the material, light on the glaze, or an architectural space. Brand and product are recognizable as soon as the page opens.
      2. Material story in three partsRaw material, process, the tile in a room. Show the real process and say which parts are video, image sequence or real-time 3D.
      3. Consistent switching between collectionsEach collection has its own scene, while product information and actions stay in predictable places.
      4. Details you can handleRotation, lighting and exploded layers where they help explain something: thickness, edges, surface.
      5. Real catalog and selectionEvery product on display links straight to its SKU, sizes, accessories, samples and room preview.
      6. Direct contact and a light modeKeep the booking link, and offer a version with images and short videos for low-end devices and for people who prefer reduced motion.

      What it takesArt direction, 3D models and material assets, storyboards, motion design and device testing. Build one collection completely first, then decide whether to extend it after testing real loading times and interaction.

      Photo try-on flow

      From one room photo to a quote you can check.

      1. Style ideas
      2. Preview with real products
      3. Quote to review

      The key differenceGenerated images help find a direction. Choosing real products needs product assets and exact specs, and a single photo does not give an accurate area.

      1. Upload a photo and select the area to change

        The customer uploads a room photo. Walls, floor and other surfaces are detected and outlined, and can be clicked, added to or erased. Offer sample rooms, and say how photos are stored, used and deleted.

      2. Choose a real SKU that is on sale

        Filter by color family, texture, finish, size, budget and where it can be used. Each preview is tied to a product and a valid combination of specs; out-of-stock and unsuitable options are clearly marked.

      3. Let AI explain and narrow the choice

        It reads the current room, the shortlisted products and the confirmed requirements. A few short questions become editable filters, and each recommendation links to official product data; missing performance data and uncertain sizes are left blank.

      4. Adjust laying pattern, grout and scale

        Set direction, grout color and joint width within the SKU’s sizes and rules, separately for walls and floors. Fix the scale from a reference dimension or calibration data, keep objects in front of the surface where they are, and allow manual correction.

      5. Compare options on the same photo

        Save two or three options and compare them from the same viewpoint, with a before/after slider. Each option holds its SKU, size, laying pattern and open questions; AI-generated inspiration is clearly labeled.

      6. Produce a materials list you can check

        Work out the number of boxes from the confirmed area, the coverage per box and a stated waste allowance, and show the calculation; trims and baseboards are counted separately. A rendered image is not an accurate quantity takeoff.

      7. Request a quote with the whole plan attached

        Photos, SKUs, choices and the materials list go into the quote request automatically; the customer adds location, timing and contact details. Stock, delivery, installation and taxes are checked against live rules or by a person.

      Research notes
      
      
      Compiled on November 21, 2026. Case links go to brand websites, public demos, studio write-ups or award records.
      

      
      
        
      Criteria
      
        
      
          
      Visual identity
      Do layout, type, color, photography and materials form one consistent language? Can you say concretely why it looks good, instead of just calling it “upscale”?
      
          
      Originality
      Does it offer a new form or interaction that means something? Tell apart original mechanisms, established industry patterns, purely visual concepts and marketing claims.
      
          
      Clarity
      Can you find the product, its specs, its limits and the next step? Is complex information layered so that the differences that matter for buying are clear?
      
          
      Interaction and usability
      Is there feedback, can the user correct what they did, is it readable on a phone? Without testing, make no claims about frame rate, loading time or conversion rate.
      
          
      Value for a tile site
      Would it improve choosing, comparing, picturing the room, organizing a project, requesting a quote or brand communication, and what assets, data and development would it take?
      
        
      
      
      

      
      
        
      
          
      How to read and verify
      
          
      
            
      Selection
      This is a shortlist for a tile project. Taste and commercial fit depend on the brand, its customers and its market.
      
            
      A brand can appear twice
      The 90 entries are counted by purpose. Floema’s product catalog and its homepage, for example, are one entry each.
      
            
      Public sources are kept apart from hands-on tests
      Sources include brand websites, feature pages, official announcements, studio write-ups and award records. Finding the entry point, watching a recording and using it yourself from start to finish are different levels of evidence. This round did not run full performance and interaction tests on every site.
      
            
      Recent work next to established cases
      Site version, launch year, article update year and award year are never mixed up; “Classic”, “Earlier version” and “Concept” are required labels.
      
          
      
        
      
        
      
          
      Limits of the technical descriptions
      
          
      
            
      Product Q&A
      Check whether it understands the current SKU, quotes reliable specs, remembers choices and hands over to a person when needed.
      
            
      Room dimensions
      AI segmentation, real material textures, AR, 3D and generative redesign are different mechanisms; only a real SKU can go on to a quote request.
      
            
      Models and video
      Video, image sequences, WebGL/WebGPU, model viewers and AR must each be described as what they are.
      
            
      Performance, privacy and accessibility need real tests
      This material is a study of design cases only. It is no guarantee for any platform, device, region or current service.
