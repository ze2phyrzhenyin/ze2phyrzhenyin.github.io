01

Executive summary

Start at Kwame Nkrumah Circle, Accra, alongside an independent phone seller and a repairer. Follow one used handset from the first inspection through payment and, if it comes back, the final remedy. The second visit should be a bale opening at Kantamanto and the tailor or downstream trader who receives the lower grades. Reserve Kumasi’s Adum for a comparison with Circle. The Ghana connection reduces the cost of learning. The strongest demand remains to be discovered.

Five findings that change the investigation

  1. 1

    Buyers already pay for trust

    A researcher met a customer who travelled 7½ hours and expected the same journey home for a warranty repair in Kumasi. The phone had cost GHS 400 and the outbound fare was GHS 35, with the same amount expected for the return. The choice reveals the value he placed on trusted parts and the cost of redeeming a promise. It is one historical case. Lu’s field account

  2. 2

    Accra already has people who check, refer and repair

    A field mapping counted 127 repair centres at Circle and documented repairers sharing expertise through WhatsApp. A third party must show what it can resolve that this network cannot. Accra cluster study

  3. 3

    Clothing quality risk is borne before the customer arrives

    Kantamanto traders commit cash to sealed bales. A 2026 study in Greater Kumasi found that material classified as waste still generated resale value. Track pieces, weight, time and cash separately. Kantamanto research design Kumasi study

  4. 4

    Refill already has a price and a practical obstacle

    Jibu lists an 18.9 L bottle at GHS 95 new and GHS 22 for a refill. An Accra customer separately recorded a different supplier declining another brand’s empties. Route density and bottle compatibility are testable reasons for a missed refill. Jibu Ghana Customer account

  5. 5

    Supply can recover faster than a trader’s cash flow

    Ghana’s recorded worn-article imports rose to USD 115.28m in 2025. A year after the Kantamanto fire, traders described weak footfall and insufficient capital to reopen. WITS GNA interviews

First month: test a paid phone check and a trader-led clothing service. Keep refill delivery as a third, conditional route. An easy mistake is to treat customs value as shoppers’ spending. The result most likely to reverse this order is how many recent phone disputes a shop could have avoided or resolved more cheaply, multiplied by who would actually pay to do so.

Accra first, Kumasi as the comparison

An existing Ghana contact can arrange introductions or interpret a negotiation. It cannot establish customer demand. The best first site combines several actors within walking distance with an observable decision involving money. The ranking below concerns the value of a visit and the plausible commercial upside of the specific service. A high score for the activity can coexist with weak evidence of a payer.

Who is missing from the first round?

A seller introduction selects people who trust that seller. Recruit at least one buyer who walked away, one without smartphone data, one woman trader outside association leadership, and a repair worker who refers jobs elsewhere. Ask each where their last transaction occurred. Separate a shop owner’s answer from an apprentice’s and a purchaser’s answer from that of the person actually using the device.

04

The customer’s last transaction

Kumasi, October 2019

Akwasi had bought a Tecno handset in Adum six months earlier for GHS 400. To use the warranty he travelled from Enchi overnight and expected to pay GHS 70 for both bus legs. Transport alone was 17.5% of the original handset price. His account attributes the trip to distrust of available local parts. It shows willingness to incur expense for one trusted repair. It cannot tell us typical willingness to pay for a certificate, or what bus fare costs today. Original field account

Kantamanto, 2022/23 field baseline

A researcher observed a buyer pay GHS 90 for jeans and GHS 15 to shorten and alter them. On this transaction the buyer spent GHS 105 to obtain usable trousers, with 14.3% going toward fit. A remote listing would have to preserve access to fitting or beat the time and money saved by obtaining it on site. One observed receipt-equivalent is more informative about who captures value than a national bale count. The source recorded observation, without a merchant ledger. FairWertung field report, graphic 7 and adjacent text

Accra water delivery, 2021 customer update

A resident describes paying GHS 45 apiece for filled YES Water bottles after the supplier declined Voltic empties. The customer records a GHS 10 refill quote and a separate GHS 52 delivered exchange of four Voltic bottles with another provider. Different supplier, terms and dates prevent a direct comparison with Jibu’s current price. The account identifies brand compatibility, directions via WhatsApp and the inconvenience of failed suppliers. Customer account

Why the cheapest offer can lose

Street stall and trusted trader

The buyer can test, bargain and find the same person tomorrow. The seller can refer a problem to a neighbouring technician. A relative may pay or refer the buyer, while another person uses the item. Informal arrangements complicate refunds when the acquaintance or stall moves. Recruit through associations and independently on the pavement. Field mapping

Group, classified, branded counter

A social group extends an existing network. A classified exposes more prices while the stranger still has to inspect, pay and collect. A counter can publish return terms, but travel, data, waiting, proof of purchase and exclusions determine their practical value. One Accra seller advertises seven-day support subject to inspection. Ask the person who declined the purchase which of these mattered. COLE policy Regional buyer remedies

How people will actually respond

Offer a paper or voice option in the language each person chooses, using Twi, Ga or English with a local interviewer as appropriate. Record cash alongside MoMo. A 2024 Accra field study conducted many informal-sector interviews in Twi. The census permits comparisons by district, age, gender and device type; it does not measure whether an individual could spare data that day. Accra research methods GSS ICT tables

05

A used handset at Circle

Follow the handset

A household or importer supplies the dealer. A local swap makes the seller of the old handset a buyer of the replacement, with a cash top-up or credit. The shop inspects, sets a purchase price and holds inventory. A technician verifies charge, connectivity, screen, cameras and owner-authorised release of account locks, and discloses replaced parts. Buyer compares, negotiates, inspects at pickup and pays. Date the receipt and explain who handles a fault. If the item comes back, log inspection, remedy, time, replaced component and onward sale, parts recovery or handover. The paths are observed in aggregate, not traced for one serial-numbered unit. Who earns and who loses

The dealer’s contribution per sale is cash received less handset acquisition, parts, test labour, payment fees, delivery, loss on returns and inventory markdown. A dealer who collects GHS 2,950 through a gateway charging 1.95% pays about GHS 57.53 before returns. A service collecting only GHS 25 would pay about GHS 0.49 on that charge. Paystack publishes this tariff; the examples do not establish that the seller uses it. A swap credit and a cash price require separate fields. Tariff

Service sensitivity, illustrative GHS per check. At a GHS 25 fee with GHS 12 technician time, GHS 2 consumables and GHS 0.49 payment cost, GHS 10.51 remains before rework and fixed costs. If one disputed check costs GHS 70, break-even dispute frequency is 15.0%. A doorstep return can exhaust the margin. Request a technician’s time sheet and the last ten resolved claims.

06

Opening a clothing bale in Kantamanto

From exporter to wearer

A sorter packs articles by description or grade. An importer and wholesaler can sell smaller lots through intermediaries. A retailer commits funds or takes credit before inspecting individual pieces. At opening, sellers sort first selections, clean or steam items, set prices, discount slower stock and refer alterations to tailors. Some pieces reach another trader, are given away or sold for low-value uses. Some ultimately enter the waste stream. Porters, including kayayei, move bales. The records needed for a service sit across these actors, each with a different claim on the proceeds. Field chain Trader interviews Kumasi material flow

An observed loss

At an October 2023 opening a trader paid GHS 2,200 for 193 pairs of women’s jeans/trousers. Researchers counted 46 at a potential GHS 25, 19 at GHS 15, 29 at GHS 3–6 and 99 rejected. Even selling every priced piece at those levels gives GHS 1,522–1,609, a GHS 591–678 shortfall before other costs. The report’s prose gives GHS 1,502 at the low end; its table and the arithmetic yield GHS 1,522. Selling prices were potential and the observed purchase was one bale. Field tally, stallholder 7

A different bale, three years later

In a May 2026 industry account, a 55 kg children’s-clothing bale is illustrated with about 165 garments and a quoted GHS 7,000–15,000 price range. UKFT uses the illustrative midpoint of GHS 11,000. Recovering only that outlay would require GHS 66.67 per item if all 165 sold, or GHS 83.33 if 132 sold and the rest had zero recovery value. Add portering, credit, alterations, stall charges and slow stock. The quoted range is one industry visitor’s account; it cannot set today’s price for a jeans or shoe bale. UKFT field visit

What happens to low grades?

A Greater Kumasi study covering 621 fashion shops and 279 bales classified 23.8% of bale weight as textile waste. The authors report resale and gifting of that material. Nairobi and Accra percentages cannot be pooled with this number. At Kantamanto, a CEPR-backed exploratory study is using bale audits to examine competing estimates, supplier incentives and quality uncertainty. Its project page describes work in progress. A comparison needs the same bale type, point in the chain, mass versus pieces and final destination. Mensah et al. PEDL project

Test a service the trader controls

Make a dated tally by grade and introduce a tailor or downstream buyer for pieces already sorted. Merchant retains ownership and approves any disclosure of terms. Net incremental value is extra cash collected plus avoided handling, less added labour, delivery, credit losses and the service fee. For illustration, if eight extra pieces each earn GHS 6 above the usual clearance offer, and transport and labour cost GHS 30, the maximum joint surplus is GHS 18 per bale. A GHS 20 fee would absorb more than the additional value.

Avoid paying for a second audit of the same bale. Ask the research team and KOBA whether anonymised grade sheets exist, then spend scarce field time following buyers and cash settlement after the bale is opened. A supplier’s change in sorting or refund terms may offer more value than showing traders a defect they already see. Research design KOBA statement

A returnable water bottle in Accra

A customer buys a full container, returns an acceptable empty and buys another fill

Jibu lists GHS 95 new and GHS 22 refill for 18.9 L, and GHS 145 new and GHS 25 refill for 20 L. The differences of GHS 73 and GHS 120 are the gaps between published prices. A cash-refund entitlement would require the provider’s deposit terms. Confirm rules at the franchise, including whether it accepts another branch’s bottle, whether wash and seal are included and who rejects a damaged container. A route threshold, not a floated business model

Let m be the margin per delivered refill after water, treatment, washing, cap, payment and expected net bottle loss, before transport. Route contribution is completed paid refills × m minus loaded route cost. If m = GHS 3 and a round costs GHS 60, 20 completed fills cover the round. At GHS 2 the threshold is 30. Both inputs are sensitivity assumptions. A start-up taking a booking fee has a different margin from the water producer. Ask the distributor for a route manifest, actual contribution and three days of rejected empties.

A real counterexample

A Ghana UNDP field experiment offered paper cups free to Koko sellers. It tests responses to a packaging substitution under subsidy. It gives little evidence about whether customers will pay a deposit or take a reusable vessel back. A brewery’s glass bottle washer, in contrast, documents an actual reuse process. Neither source measures repeat paid water fills for Jibu. Ghana’s L.I. 2250 sets return and refund rules where an established deposit scheme applies. Its existence does not establish a nationwide consumer deposit for all PET. Regulations UNDP field experiment Glass wash and inspection

Give refill a field day if one distributor can show a repeat route, a specific loss and a person authorised to spend against it. If it runs well with a driver’s notebook and losses are low, record that result and leave the route alone.

A bottom-up, editable-in-practice estimate

For a pilot catchment, suppose 10–25 willing phone shops each handle 8–20 eligible handovers per month. That gives 80–500 eligible transactions, before adoption. If 20–40% take a paid check, the reachable volume is 16–200 checks/month. At GHS 15–30 per check, gross service revenue would be GHS 240–6,000/month. A middle scenario is 15 × 12 × 30% × GHS 25 = GHS 1,350 from 54 checks. These are field-planning assumptions, not measured demand, and exclude fixed costs.

For textiles, 4–10 accessible retailers × 2–6 eligible bales/month = 8–60 bales. At 25–50% paid take-up and GHS 10–20 per completed service, revenue is GHS 20–600/month. Low initial scale makes direct selling expensive. For bottles, one partner’s 2–5 routes × 10–25 completed paid fills × 4 observed route days yields 80–500 fills over those four days. Price a distributor service on independently measured savings, not the retail value of the water.

Measure first: preventable, resolvable loss in GHS per participating shop per week, together with the number of eligible handovers. A verified willingness to pay at a real handover converts this figure into a business estimate.

09

What three markets can teach the Ghana team

Kenya · Nairobi

Badili’s advertised remedy changes over time: a qualifying technical defect brings replacement in the first seven days, parts and labour repair cover in months 1–6, then customer-paid parts and covered labour in months 7–12. A buyer who lives far from the service point bears another cost. KNBS reports second-hand clothing imports of 183,830 tonnes in 2021, 230,535 in 2024 and 250,527 in 2025. Import value rose from KSh 27.81bn to 30.26bn in 2024–25, while nominal import value per tonne changed little. This measures supply. Gikomba shoe sellers’ displacement in March 2026 demonstrates that delivery or pickup nodes can change abruptly. Transfer: publish fault-specific cover. Failure condition: a return trip or unstocked part makes cover uneconomic for the buyer. Badili KNBS tables 6.6, 6.8, 6.10 Gikomba reporting

Côte d’Ivoire · Abidjan

DOPA MULTISERVICE in Angré–Plateau Dokui listed an iPhone 11 128 GB described as « propre », with Face ID, receipt and guarantee, at 117,000 FCFA on 16 October 2025 and 115,000 FCFA on 24 October. The first offer mentioned exchange. These same-seller asks have closer specification than cross-country medians. We cannot establish they concern a single physical unit or that either came with a paid claim. « Occasion », « venu de France », « reconditionné », « troc » and « garantie » are useful interview terms. Transfer: record the exact receipt and remedy. Failure condition: a Ghanaian currency price or grading vocabulary obscures the actual model, repairs or recourse. Start in French alongside a local trader and ask what buyers call the grade. 16 October listing 24 October listing Consumer law

South Africa · urban fashion resale

Yaga’s 16 November 2026 guide says a buyer usually pays about 5% of applicable order value plus a fixed amount, with the exact fee at checkout. Its user agreement sets the window for reporting issues. The press kit reports more than six million items sold across the platform’s markets, without a South African cohort or audited take rate. November terms and a dated payment promotion show a maintained product. Transfer: allocate delivery, claim deadlines, cash release and fault ownership explicitly. Failure condition: a low-ticket garment cannot absorb two parcel journeys, or a buyer lacks timely pickup. Test Yaga’s logic against the GHS 15 tailoring service at Kantamanto. Fees Terms Press kit Payment promotion

Why Nigeria remains a comparator

GSMA’s consumer survey found 18% of surveyed Nigerian consumers and 14% of surveyed South Africans had acquired their current phone used. Ghana was absent from that sub-Saharan comparison. Nigeria’s NCC offers a device-status check. These are reasons to study verification at Lagos counters, while Côte d’Ivoire adds a nearer francophone comparison and South Africa adds a different fulfillment model. An identifier check does not establish battery health or lawful seller title in every circumstance. GSMA survey NCC guidance

Where money could enter Manna

The merchant might buy a check to avoid a disputed sale, the buyer might buy independent advice, or a distributor might pay for fewer empty-container losses. These are competing hypotheses. Record who proposed the service, who actually paid and who retained the upside. A grant can finance learning while leaving all three questions unresolved.

Operating evidence and dead ends

Local phone supply

Valmont publishes stock and counter terms; its public swap board also reported no listings at the check. COLE distinguishes locally traded devices from imports; neither source discloses paid volumes or settled claims. University of Ghana fieldwork in Osu, Nima and Airport found about 61% of 269 respondents answering a used-phone question said they would not buy used. A separate 107-response table highlighted desired features, lower prices and knowing the previous owner. This is historical intention from selected neighbourhoods. Recruit cash buyers of less expensive Androids and people who decided to buy new. Valmont board COLE Ghana household study

A promotion with a known end

M-KOPA, Samsung and Franko launched a Ghana phone swap for selected A-series devices in 2024. The Samsung terms specify 20 May–30 December 2024. Up to GHS 950 for an A-grade A14 128 GB versus GHS 810 at grade B was a historic credit schedule. It shows how one merchant-funded programme differentiated condition. Subsequent financing activity cannot establish that this offer was renewed. Request redemptions, rejected handsets and post-sale claims from the partners. Launch Exact dates, models and trade-in table

The cost of delivery can close a channel

Glovo told Ghana restaurant partners it would exit in May 2024, citing difficulty achieving profitability. It sold delivery, not specifically surplus food. The case matters to a proposed markdown service because the same rider can cost more than a low-price basket earns. In Kenya, KOKO’s refill network suffered a shutdown in early 2026. The UK entity’s administrator provides stronger evidence of corporate distress than stale product pages. Energy refill economics involved carbon finance and cannot be transferred wholesale to drinking water. Determine who funds the service if an external revenue stream disappears. Glovo partner reporting KOKO administrator

Three routes for surplus food

Food for All Africa’s network reports 249,165 kg distributed and 33,881 people served in calendar 2025. Its documented supply route includes farmers, cold storage and community distribution. These are service outputs. AkoFresh describes solar cold storage and market access around Kumasi; its impact figures are company-reported and its public site does not establish the number of paying repeat customers. What changes the cost of a sale

Ghana · used stock and e-waste

Environmental Protection Act 2025 (Act 1124), assented to on 6 January 2025, consolidates the regime formerly associated with Act 917. Sections 124–125 distinguish equipment import conditions from controls on waste; §139 addresses take-back and §143 includes functional condition in the classification. Obtain the specific importer registration, permit, fee and receiving facility record for a proposed shipment. Basel’s e-waste amendments took effect on 1 January 2025 for bound parties and expand prior informed consent for cross-border e-waste. Used equipment must be classified on its facts and under relevant national rules. An advertisement saying “home used” establishes little. The November 2026 investigation of UK returns in Accra illustrates the gap between law and practice. Act 1124 Basel FAQ Investigation

Ghana · product-specific restrictions

L.I. 1932 regulates import and sale of imported used refrigerators, freezers and air conditioners. Domestic repair of a working family refrigerator is a different activity. GSA also lists specific prohibited second-hand goods and conditions under L.I. 1586, including selected personal items. A trader cannot apply the same import rule to an entire mixed container of apparel and household goods. Ask the Energy Commission or Customs for the classification of the proposed goods and recent inspection practice. L.I. 1932 Port inspections, 2024 GSA inspection

Ghana · remedies, data and food

Sale of Goods Act 1962 §13 concerns defects and fitness with qualifications, including disclosed defects and examination. For electronic transactions, Act 772 §49 provides a fourteen-day cancellation rule for covered goods and exceptions including certain auctions and everyday food deliveries. A shop’s voluntary seven-day policy does not settle every purchaser’s statutory rights. The broad consumer bill was still being pressed for in October 2026; enactment was not verified by the cutoff. The Data Protection Commission requires controller registration before processing personal data under Act 843. Keep field counts anonymous where possible. For a food service, check FDA permits and handling requirements with the local operator. Act 137 Act 772 Bill status reporting DPC FDA

Kenya · producer duties

NEMA publishes EPR implementation guidance for the 2024 regulations. In November 2025 a court lifted its earlier interim orders. A separate July 2026 interim ruling protected RETRAK members dealing only in sealed third-party goods from specified upstream obligations. An October draft ministry strategy describes enforcement as suspended while NEMA’s November FAQ describes import certification. Confirm scope for the precise operator in writing. July ruling Ministry draft NEMA FAQ A point-of-sale deposit requires a settlement contract even where producer responsibility exists. Gazette Court order NEMA

Côte d’Ivoire and South Africa

Ivorian consumer law 2016-412 governs consumer supply and specified distance sales; official customs texts implement the e-waste regime. Ask a dealer for a resolved warranty case and a collector for a signed handover. South Africa requires an import-permit route for used goods, and Consumer Protection Act §§55–57 provides remedies for applicable supplier transactions, including qualifying defects within six months. A casual private sale needs separate analysis. Do not equate Yaga’s platform dispute window with the full statutory position. Ivorian law Douanes ITAC South African Act

What to do in the next 30 days

Days 1–5 · establish the comparison

With local introductions, walk two time blocks at Circle and one at Adum, or arrange the Adum comparison by phone first. Draw the routes and count distinct open shops, including pavement stalls. Invite 8 phone businesses, 2 technicians outside the sellers’ referrals, 6 clothing traders and 2 tailors or lower-grade buyers. Ask two refill franchisees whether one can share anonymous route totals. Include one evening or weekend session where permitted. Ask permission before observing a transaction. A research participant’s compensation is never counted as revenue.

Days 6–12 · establish what happens now

Shadow at least 20 eligible handset handovers and ten recent faults or complaints, including cases resolved free. Record the original seller, actual price, buyer and user, grade disagreements, remedy, direct cost, time and missing records. At Kantamanto follow at least four bales of the same broad category across two traders, then revisit the lower grades. Ask about a failed sale. At Adum reprice one matched part and one fault. Four bales can reveal a process, not estimate the market’s waste percentage.

Days 13–22 · run a small paid choice

Offer the same clearly priced manual check during successive eligible handovers at participating shops. Alternate comparable time blocks with normal practice and log every decline. Keep the receipt private; tell the buyer who can resolve a fault. For textiles, test one paid introduction or alteration against the trader’s usual buyer for similar items, charging only on agreed terms. Do not buy inventory to manufacture adoption. If a water distributor can reconcile receipts, observe consecutive routes and offer one simple change to pickup scheduling. The startup should fund research time independently of participants’ buying decisions.

Circle + Adum · buyer, independent seller, repairer · condition and remedy

Continue at day 30

At least 6 paid checks in 30 eligible offers across 3 independent shops, at least 2 shops request another round, and contribution positive after technician time and expected rework. Inspect whether buyers or merchants paid.

Adjust, or stop

If buyers value checks but decline the fee, test a dealer-paid dispute service. Stop if no one pays after 30 properly offered handovers or existing checks solve the same cases more cheaply.

Kantamanto · retailer, tailor, low-grade buyer · offloading the next grade

Continue at day 30

At least 2 traders repeat a paid service and ≥3 of 4 matched batches yield positive incremental cash after labour, carriage and normal clearance value. Revisit on days 7 and 14.

Adjust, or stop

Shift toward an existing downstream buyer if fragmented supply is the problem. Stop if gains disappear after labour, there is no repeat payer or disclosure harms supplier relationships.

Accra refill route · franchisee, rider, returning household · missed empties

Continue at day 30

Proceed only with readable route records, positive contribution at measured density, fewer missed paid exchanges on comparable routes and a distributor willing to pay for another period.

Adjust, or stop

Try pickup or a narrower route if delivery fails. Stop if bottle incompatibility or hygiene rules preclude the change, or the route owner cannot identify savings it would pay for.

Days 23–30 · decide

Call consenting early buyers back at 7 and 14 days. Keep follow-up windows explicit. Report outcomes as “6 of 30 offered”, not “market adoption 20%”. Review failures with a non-participating technician and a trader outside the founding network. Select one payer and one repeated transaction for the next month, or pause all three. Plan day-90 durability follow-up for any continuing case.

Questions that surface actual payments

  1. Tell me about the last time you bought, sold, repaired or returned one of these. Where did it start, and what did you do next?
  2. Who would use the item? Who chose the seller, who paid, and how was the money transferred?
  3. What price was first mentioned? What changed hands in the end, including any top-up, delivery, credit, alteration or previous item?
  4. How did you decide what condition it was in? What could you check there, and what did you only learn later?
  5. Which other shop, person or option did you consider that day? What happened when you last tried that option?
  6. Think of the last deal that did not go ahead. At what point did someone walk away, and what happened to the item afterward?
  7. When a problem last appeared after payment, who did you contact? How many visits did it take, who covered each expense, and how did it end?
  8. What happened to the pieces, parts, food or containers that remained? Who picked them up, who paid whom, and how do you know where they went?
  9. Could you show me any record of that transaction with the names covered? Which costs or promises would that record leave out?

Keep an evidence trail that can survive a disagreement

For each anonymous case log the date, location, source of introduction, payer, user and usual alternative. Split advertised price, negotiated cash, store credit and money actually received. Record item specification, tested faults, promised remedy, actual outcome, hours of labour and each journey. Log missing fields as unknown. Mark direct observation, receipt, respondent recall, company claim and researcher assumption separately.

Measure whether a purchase of a new good was actually displaced. At follow-up ask what the buyer did with the original item and what would have happened without the deal. A study whose denominator is people interviewed cannot establish a repair success rate without following devices. Ask permission separately before collecting any contact information used for follow-up. Data Protection Commission
