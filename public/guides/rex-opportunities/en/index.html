Our digital business covers custom software, websites, web and mobile applications, business systems, warehouse and stock management, device inspection, work orders, document automation, systems integration and the use of AI in company processes. We have real projects and use cases.
Use both the company website and the background I have explicitly added. Do not overlook my partner’s agriculture and overseas warehousing experience because the website mainly presents the digital business.

2. Choosing how to take part according to our actual status

For each key event, judge in which capacity we are best placed to attend:
- Representative of a company registered in France.
- Supplier of software, AI and digital systems.
- Participant or operator in the agricultural business.
- Participant in warehousing, logistics and cross-border trade.
- University student and student entrepreneur.
- Startup team with a real product and case studies.
- Partner able to co-develop, run a pilot or deliver jointly.

Say whether it suits me, my partner or both of us together.
Verify personal nationality, country of company registration, sector qualifications and project eligibility separately. Do not assume that a French partner, a French SAS or student status meets every application condition.
Recommend trade-buyer channels only where there is real purchasing responsibility and a real purchasing need. Where there is no evidence of legal position, shareholding, turnover, incubation record, startup eligibility or product maturity, state clearly that it is unknown; do not count another entity’s business income as REX’s.

3. Our goals

Cover all of the following:
1. Find companies likely to buy software, AI, automation and digital services, and aim for needs interviews, demos, pilots and paid projects.
2. Apply for talks, case-study presentations, workshops, product demos, startup pitches or stands, to build credibility.
3. Look for industry partners, equipment makers, systems integrators, distributors and joint delivery teams.
4. Look for investors, incubators, accelerators, university startup resources and related support programmes.

Give priority to opportunities that can turn into real business or partnerships. Judge separately how fundable custom development services and scalable products are; do not rank by how famous an event is.

Search for events in the six months from the date of the run. At the top of the report, state the search date, the search cut-off time and the period covered, using the Europe/Paris time zone.
Remove the distance limit around Toulouse. Cover all of France, Spain, Germany and Belgium, and add other European destinations such as the Netherlands according to commercial value; keep low-cost local opportunities in and around Toulouse.
For distant events, explain why the trip is worth making. A small number of online events that connect directly with clients or partners in these regions may be added.
When an event falls outside the six months but its application deadline is close, list it only under a separate heading, “early application reminders”, and keep it out of the main list.

5. Sectors and event formats

Cover manufacturing, machinery, electronics, aerospace, production management, quality inspection and maintenance; agriculture, fruit and vegetables, livestock, farm machinery, cooperatives and food processing; warehousing, transport, packaging, cross-border supply chains, e-commerce, refurbishment and recycling; hotels and catering, tourism and transport, education, retail, construction and property, energy, and other sectors with a real need for software.
Do not search only tech conferences and startup competitions, and do not pad the list with events just to cover a sector.
Event formats include trade shows, supply-and-demand matchmaking, open technology challenges, open innovation, calls for pilots, chamber of commerce and trade association meetings, business breakfasts, business matchmaking, calls for speakers, stands and joint pavilions, investor pitches, university startup events and tech communities.
Focus on whether we can reach company heads, factory or farm operators, warehouse operators, and people responsible for production, purchasing, IT and operations.

Go online yourself and search in French and English, and in Spanish and Catalan where needed.
